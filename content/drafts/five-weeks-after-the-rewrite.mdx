A year of running this product has taught me to distrust clean wins, so let me start with the messy answer I promised.

In May I wrote about StudyPDF's one unsolved problem. People came for an exam, used it hard for two weeks, passed, and disappeared. I ended that post like this:

> I have hypotheses, no answers. I'll write that one when I have something honest to say.

Then I did the least cautious thing possible and rewrote the entire product around that problem. The why and the what are in [the previous post](/blog/the-bo-rewrite). Five weeks later I have a first answer. It is early, and the most interesting part of it is something I got wrong.

One caveat before the numbers. I started the rewrite in mid-May and it went live on June 1. So this is an early read, not a verdict, and the product is deliberately not in a marketing push yet. I will get to why. But the first signal is already clear enough to write down.

---

## The finding

Users who create even one study artifact in their first week come back at about 5.7 times the rate of users who don't.

In plain numbers: 22.4% of artifact creators returned in week one, against 3.9% of people who signed up and never made one.

![Week-one return rate, creators versus non-creators: 22.4% against 3.9%](/blog/bo-relaunch/chart-02-creators-vs-noncreators.svg)

I did not fully trust that the first time I saw it, so I ran it a second way, from a different data source, with a stricter definition of "returned." Not just "loaded a page again" but "came back and actually generated something." On that stricter measure the gap is wider. 12.4% of creators against 1.2% of non-creators, roughly ten to one. Two sources, same direction, same size. And it does not wash out after a week. Into week two the relative gap holds, even as the absolute numbers get small. I will come back to that at the end, because it matters.

---

## The part I got wrong

Going in, I was sure of one thing. The mastery layer, the per-student memory I bet the rewrite on, would lift retention across the board. Day-one retention would climb because the product now knew each user better.

It didn't. Top-line day-one retention stayed flat, around 8%, before and after.

For a few days that reading felt like the bet had missed. Then the creators-versus-non-creators split made it click. Retention was never actually broken. It is conditional. Once someone reaches their first artifact, the product holds onto them fine. The problem was never that people who used Bo failed to come back. The problem is how few people reach that first artifact at all.

![The funnel: most signups never reach a first artifact, but the ones who do come back](/blog/bo-relaunch/chart-07-activation-funnel.svg)

Only about 28% of signups ever make a first artifact. That is the leak. I had spent the rewrite solving the back half of the funnel, retention, when the front half, activation, was the half actually losing people. I built a better bucket, and most people never reached the tap.

---

## What that reframed

So the work pivoted. Instead of making the memory smarter, I went after the first-artifact gap. Auto-creating a first course for new users. An onboarding that pushes you to upload before anything else. Less standing between a new signup and Bo making them something.

In a clean two-week window, that lifted activation (signup to first upload) from roughly 55% to about 69%. It is the most useful 15 points I have moved in a while, and it came entirely from accepting that I had been optimizing the wrong half of the funnel.

---

## The scale this is running at

For context on the numbers above: StudyPDF went from 149 users in August 2024 to over 92,000 today. About 7,700 of those joined in the last month, 1,600 in the last week. Monthly active users grew more than six times over, from about 2,200 in December 2025 to about 14,700 in May 2026. There are 399 paying customers and a large, growing free base beneath them.

![Monthly active users, December 2025 to May 2026, climbing from about 2,200 to 14,700](/blog/bo-relaunch/chart-03-mau-6x.svg)

The detail I keep coming back to. The two biggest signup months in the product's history were the two right around the rewrite, April and May, each adding roughly 8,000 new users. I tore the product down to the studs at the steepest part of the growth curve, and growth did not flinch. That is either reckless or correct, and I will not know which for a while.

One thing did flinch, and it is worth naming. The product-market-fit survey score dropped to a scary low the week of the cutover, then climbed back over the following weeks. Tearing down a live product that people are mid-semester depending on is not free, even when the signup curve hides the cost. For a few days I was watching the one number that would have told me to stop, and it was falling.

One more thing the data said plainly: people come to test themselves. In the first 19 days, students made 3,268 artifacts, and quizzes plus exams together (1,254 of them) outnumbered study guides. That matches what they had been telling me in surveys all along. The thing they wanted most was to be quizzed, not just summarized.

---

## What I'm not doing yet

The product is ready to push. I am not pushing it yet.

I am letting retention and activation settle for a few clean weeks before I turn acquisition back up. There is no point pouring new users into a bucket until I have watched it hold water on its own. I ran a daily retention watch from June 7 to 18 with two gates. Did activation actually lift, and is the new cohort retaining at least as well as the old product. Both came back green on June 18. That is permission to start, not proof that it is solved. So I am watching a little longer before I open the tap.

---

## What's still unsolved

I want to be straight about the size of this. The relative lift is huge. The absolute long tail is still thin. On the strict week-two measure, creators return at 3.8% and non-creators at 0.2%. A sixteen-to-one ratio, and still single digits. Plenty of people make one artifact, feel the value, and still do not come back the next week.

And the original problem from the last post, the one that started all of this, is not solved. Students still come for an exam and leave when it is over. Cross-semester retention is still close to zero. What changed is that I understand the shape of it now. It is not a wall I can patch by adding a feature. It is a funnel, the leak sits earlier than I thought, and the part below the leak finally holds.

That is more than I had to say last time, which was nothing. It is less than "solved." I will write the next one when the few clean weeks are up and I can tell you whether the bucket held.
