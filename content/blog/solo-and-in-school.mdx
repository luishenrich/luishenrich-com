A friend asked me last week whether running StudyPDF and doing a Master&rsquo;s at RWTH at the same time has been &ldquo;worth it.&rdquo; The honest answer is *I don&rsquo;t know yet*, but I have collected a few observations along the way.

## You will do both badly

Not catastrophically. Just enough that, on any given Tuesday, you can locate the seams. The lab report would have been sharper with another evening. The product email would have been better with another draft. You ship anyway, because the alternative is shipping nothing.

I&rsquo;ve made peace with this. Some weeks one side gets the strong version of me and the other gets the polite minimum. The math only works if you&rsquo;re honest about which is which.

## The default mode is context-switching, and it&rsquo;s expensive

The romanticized version of this life is *quick context switches* — write some code, attend a lecture, answer support, finish a problem set. In practice it&rsquo;s closer to: the lecture pulls a fraction of your attention because there&rsquo;s a Stripe alert in the corner of your eye, and the support reply is half-baked because a problem set is due in three hours.

What worked, eventually, was blocking days. Not hours — days. Tuesday and Thursday are university days, full stop. The rest is StudyPDF. The cost of being fully present in one thing is much lower than the cost of being half-present in two.

## Don&rsquo;t hide one from the other

For a long time I treated the company as a thing my classmates didn&rsquo;t need to know about, and treated school as a thing my users didn&rsquo;t need to know about. The hiding was more work than the disclosure ever would have been.

The classmates mostly think it&rsquo;s cool. The users mostly don&rsquo;t care. The only person who was making it weird was me.

## The thing nobody warns you about

Money. Specifically, what it does to your relationships in a peer group of 22-year-olds.

I&rsquo;m not rich, but the company pays my rent and my classmates&rsquo; companies do not. That asymmetry sits uneasily in friendships that were forged when everyone was equally broke. There&rsquo;s no clean way to handle it. You learn to keep certain numbers out of conversations and to pick up the dinner check sometimes and not others.

I don&rsquo;t have great advice here. Just: it&rsquo;s a thing, and it&rsquo;s worth noticing it&rsquo;s a thing before someone you care about points it out.

## Would I recommend it

To a specific kind of person, yes. The kind who would otherwise be building a company *and* feeling guilty about not being in school, or in school *and* feeling guilty about not building anything. Doing both removes the guilt by removing the choice.

To anyone else: probably not. The opportunity cost on each side is real. You should at least know what you&rsquo;re trading away.
